About Atrios · Founder's story

I started Atrios because I was drowning in FOMO.

Every day, a new tool. Every day, another cold call. There had to be a better way.

Taylor OfferFOUNDER · ATRIOSPortrait with a sealed recommendation, oil on canvas
I

Drowning

Every morning there was another launch. Another "this changes everything." Another tool I was supposedly already behind on.

A new AI agent. A new CRM. New ways to do payroll, banking, sales, hiring and accounting. And I knew it mattered, because a company is only as good as the tools it runs on.

So which ones are actually good?

Nobody could tell me. Ads said everything was the best. Review sites felt bought. The "top 10" lists were written by the tools on them. I didn't have time to demo everything, and I was terrified of picking wrong.

II

Then came the spam

The moment I looked like a buyer, the noise got louder. Cold calls during my meetings. Ten-step email sequences. LinkedIn messages that opened with "Hope this finds you well." Calendar invites from people I'd never met.

Cold callsSpam sequencesPushy demos"Just bumping this up"

None of it helped me decide. All of it made me trust vendors less.

The sales machine was broken, and everyone knew it.

III

Follow the money

Here's the part that made me angry. Companies spend a fortune trying to reach you: SDRs, ads, cold-email tools, conference booths, lead lists. Most of it is spent annoying the exact people they want as customers.

What if that money went to you instead?

Not to another ad. Not to another cold call. To you, the person actually deciding which tools your company runs on.

V

The future of sales doesn't suck

We believe sales should be people-first.

The best tools shouldn't win because they sent the most emails. They should win because people who use them recommend them. Buyers should be respected, not hunted. And when your time creates value for a company, you should share in it.
